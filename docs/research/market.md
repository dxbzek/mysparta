# Market Landscape Research: Comparables, Trends & Monetisation Benchmarks
### For an original browser RPG inspired by MyBrute (2009) / LaBrute on Eternaltwin

*Research date: 2026-08-01. Focus: comparable-game teardowns, the async auto-battler renaissance, browser-game trends 2024–2026, retention benchmarks, ethical F2P monetisation, and the opportunity gap.*

---

## 1. Context: the MyBrute lineage

MyBrute (Motion Twin, 2009) pioneered the "async fighting" formula: create a character in seconds, get randomly-rolled stats/weapons/skills/pets, watch fully automated fights vs other players' brutes, recruit pupils via referral links. Motion Twin (a French worker cooperative founded 2001, equal salary and decision-making) shut down all its web games on 2 November 2023 but released assets to support fan recreations. The community **Eternaltwin** project now runs **LaBrute** at brute.eternaltwin.org / labrute.eternaltwin.org as a free fan remake — proof of persistent demand, but it is a preservation project, not a modern commercial product. That leaves the formula's commercial territory effectively unoccupied.

## 2. Comparable game teardowns

### 2.1 The direct descendants (browser idle-RPG PvP)

**Shakes & Fidget (Playa Games, 2009, Germany)**
- *Core loop:* click-driven quests with wait timers ("partially an idle game"), arena PvP vs other players' stat sheets, guilds, dungeons. Based on a WoW-parody webcomic; won Deutscher Entwicklerpreis Best Casual Game 2009, GameStar Best Browser Game 2010. Still live on browser + Steam + mobile.
- *Monetisation:* dual currency — gold (earned) and **mushrooms** (premium). Mushrooms buy quest-energy expansion (F2P gets 100 energy, payers 300), shop items, skips. Also runs offer walls (Digital Turbine FairBid case study: +276% monthly revenue).
- *What works:* 15+ years of longevity, snackable sessions, comic tone, server-based fresh-start ladders.
- *What players hate:* widely called "heavily Pay2Win" — payers progress "at least 2x faster"; nearly every shop item costs mushrooms; cheapest package criticised (£9.99 for 130 mushrooms). Its P2W reputation is the single biggest lesson: energy-gating + premium-currency-everywhere poisons leaderboard credibility.

**Hero Zero (Playata, 2012/2016 Steam, "35M+ players")**
- *Core loop:* zero-to-superhero fantasy; time-based missions, training stats in your garage, animated PvP duels for honor/rank, teams with HQ battles. Essentially Shakes & Fidget re-skinned with superhero comedy.
- *Monetisation:* free-to-play with premium currency (donuts) via a separate payments entity; timers + speed-ups.
- *Reception:* mixed — 63% positive of ~600 Steam reviews; same P2W/timer complaints. Demonstrates the formula still retains players for a decade despite dated monetisation.

**Swords & Souls: Neverseen (SoulGame, Armor Games — Flash original was a MyBrute-adjacent hit)**
- *Core loop:* train stats via **rhythm/reaction minigames** (each stat has its own minigame that scales in difficulty), then turn-based auto-ish combat, earn money, upgrade town/gear, repeat.
- *What works:* training-as-gameplay gives *agency over stat growth* — the thing MyBrute lacked entirely.
- *What players hate:* repetition; minigames and combat "become stale quickly." Lesson: minigame training is a great spice but a weak core.

**Torn (Chedburn Networks, 2004, text-based crime MMO)**
- *Core loop:* energy/nerve-based actions (crimes, gym training, attacks), deep player-driven economy, factions, real-time politics. Sessions are minutes long, but the world is persistent and social obligations are heavy.
- *Numbers:* 48,515 DAU in Jan 2024 → **crossed 100,000 DAU on 27 April 2026**, its first six-figure day in 22 years (growth credited to infrastructure + advertising/PR).
- *Monetisation:* **Donator Pack** — $5 one-off item granting 31 days donator status + 60 points; **subscription $4.85/mo** (donator status, monthly items, extra points, subscriber draw). Crucially, Donator Packs are *tradeable in-game*, so free players can buy them with in-game cash — an EVE-PLEX-style bridge that launders whale spend into F2P goodwill.
- *Lesson:* a browser game with 2004 graphics can double DAU in 2024–26 on the strength of economy depth, identity, and a fair supporter-tier model.

**Melvor Idle (Games by Malcs; published by Jagex)**
- *Core loop:* RuneScape-inspired idle skilling; check-in sessions; runs in browser/PWA/Steam/mobile.
- *Monetisation:* **premium buy-once** (~$10) + paid expansions; explicit "no microtransactions… level playing field" philosophy carried into Melvor Idle 2. Estimated ~$6.7M gross on Steam alone; grew from solo dev to a 4-person team.
- *Lesson:* a solo-dev browser-first idle game can clear millions with buy-once + expansions and zero IAP — the audience rewards clean monetisation with trust and word of mouth.

**AFK Journey (Lilith, March 2024) — the mid-core ceiling**
- *Core loop:* idle RPG + autochess-style positional combat, Genshin-adjacent art; >12M downloads and $128M IAP in ~7 months, >$185M lifetime; Google Play & App Store Best Game 2024.
- *Monetisation:* traditional "solid token" gacha + layered passes/subscriptions; heavy social subsystems for retention.
- *Lesson:* proves the *idle + auto-battle + build* audience is huge — but its gacha model is exactly what an ethical MyBrute successor should define itself against.

### 2.2 The async auto-battler renaissance (the key genre shift)

These three games are, mechanically, MyBrute's grandchildren: build asynchronously, fight ghosts, no reflexes required. What they added is **drafting/economy agency** — the player *earns* their build instead of receiving it from RNG at level-up.

**Super Auto Pets (Team Wood Games, 2021 — browser/itch.io first, then Steam/mobile)**
- *Loop:* shop phase (buy/sell/combine pets and food with per-turn gold) → async auto-battle vs a *recorded ghost* of another player at the same win-count; race to 10 wins before losing all hearts. Runs 5–15 min, **pausable mid-run with no penalty**.
- *Monetisation benchmark:* F2P; expansion packs $5–10 (all-packs bundle ~$30); a **free rotating "weekly pack"** lets everyone sample paid pets; almost all cosmetics earnable via play points. Widely cited as "a benchmark for how mobile F2P can work when monetization stays out of the way." 89% positive on 35k+ Steam reviews. Main community gripe: content (pets) being the paid element rather than cosmetics.
- *Design additions over MyBrute:* drafting economy, team-order positioning, buff-food itemisation, matchmaking by win-count ladders.

**Backpack Battles (PlayWithFurcifer, EA 8 March 2024)**
- *Loop:* buy items → **spatially arrange them in a grid backpack** (adjacency/star connections define synergies) → async auto-battle vs another player's build. Ten wins wins the run.
- *Numbers:* **640,000 copies in the first month** (China 48%, Japan 11%, US 10% — Japan was the top wishlist country, extremely rare); ~$9.4M gross to date; a two-person studio. Growth was driven by a *permanent generous demo* — players with thousands of demo hours still converted at $13 because they felt they'd already gotten their money's worth.
- *Design additions:* build expression through spatial puzzle; crafting/combining items into recipes; class-based starting kits.
- *Lesson:* async PvP + tinkerable builds + demo generosity = viral in 2024. Also: premium ($13) worked; no live-service needed.

**The Bazaar (Tempo/Reynad, open beta 2024, Steam launch Aug 2025) — cautionary tale**
- *Loop:* roguelike runs across in-game "days"; each day = 6 hours: hours 0/1/3/4 merchants & events, hour 2 a PvE monster fight, hour 5 an async PvP battle vs a **ghost from the same stage of another player's run**. Board is a sizing puzzle (small/medium/large items on a 10-slot board). Ranked ghosts are tougher; win-streak chests give cosmetics + currency for heroes/vanity.
- *Monetisation arc:* March 5, 2025 open-beta patch removed free ranked tickets, charged ~100 gems (~$1) per ranked run, put 10-card hero expansions solely inside a **$10/month "Prize Pass"** — after years of promising cosmetic-first monetisation. Community exploded; press called it "a brilliant game undermined by predatory monetization"; Reynad called the feedback "reliably dishonest" and fought the subreddit. After a month of damage, Tempo reversed course (expansions unlocked, paid pass removed) → "WE ARE SO BACK." The Steam launch was still shadowed by the trust damage.
- *Lessons:* (1) the PvE/PvP day-cycle structure is state-of-the-art for async runs; (2) monetising *ranked access* or *power content* in this genre triggers instant revolt; (3) trust, once promised, is a contract.

## 3. Browser gaming trends 2024–2026

**The web-game renaissance is real and measured.** Naavik ("Web Gaming Strikes Back") and Metaplay ("Return of the Web") document HTML5 portals at scale: **Poki ~60M MAU, CrazyGames ~35M MAU** (combined "95 million players a month" per Hacker News discussion of the Naavik piece); browser-games market ~$7.7B (2024) → ~$9B projected by 2029. Drivers: post-Flash tooling finally matured, app-store fee avoidance (Apple's anti-steering losses), and discovery fatigue on mobile stores.

**Distribution channels a new browser RPG can stack:**
- **Portals:** Poki, CrazyGames (rev-share on ads + growing IAP support; CrazyGames leads on IAP adoption), itch.io for early community.
- **Discord Activities:** Embedded App SDK launched March 2024; **opened to all developers Sept 2024** with IAP (one-time + subscription SKUs, entitlements, analytics) for US/UK/EU devs on desktop. Discord: 200M+ MAU. Early winners: Death by AI, Krunker Strike FRVR, Farm Merge Valley. A social auto-battler that runs inside a Discord voice channel is a natural fit for a MyBrute successor (referral/pupil mechanics map directly onto servers).
- **Telegram Mini Apps:** 950M+ Telegram accounts, 500M+ mini-app users; Hamster Kombat claimed 300M players, Notcoin 35M+ (6.5M DAU) in 2024. Mostly crypto tap-to-earn froth, but it proved *instant-load, no-install, socially-forwarded* games acquire users at near-zero CAC.
- **YouTube Playables / native "playables"** are additional zero-install surfaces.
- **PWA installability:** Melvor Idle's browser/PWA/Steam/mobile spread is the template — one codebase, installable to home screen, no store tax on web.

**Tech maturity:** WebAssembly is universal and within ~10% of native performance; WebGPU shipped in Chrome/Edge, then Safari 26 (Sept 2025), reaching Baseline across all major browsers in Nov 2025; Unity's web export and Godot/Bevy/PlayCanvas/Three.js all target it. A 2D auto-battler needs none of the bleeding edge — meaning it can hit *every* surface (portal iframe, Discord, Telegram, PWA) with instant load, which is itself a competitive weapon.

## 4. Retention benchmarks (casual/mid-core F2P)

From GameAnalytics 2024/2025 benchmark reports (11,600 games, 1.48B MAU) and aggregators:

| Metric | All-games average | Top quartile / "healthy" target |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | 26.5–27.7% | 31–33% iOS, 25–27% Android; healthy 30–40% |
| D7 | ~8% (top 25%: 7–8%; bottom 25%: ~1.5%) | healthy 10–20% |
| D30 | <3% | healthy 5–10% |

Genre notes: board/card/puzzle/casino retain best long-term; mid-core games log the most sessions/day (6–7 — relevant because a 2-minute fight loop is naturally multi-session). A MyBrute successor should aim above card-game averages: D1 ≥35%, D7 ≥15%, D30 ≥8% would be top-decile and is plausible given async PvP's "check your fights" pull.

**Mechanics that drive these numbers:**
- **Daily rewards/dailies:** used by ~95% of games to build habit, but research (PocketGamer.biz "Carrots and sticks") warns login bonuses alone are "cosmetic improvements" — the *content* of the daily loop must be intrinsically fun (MyBrute's genius: your 3–6 daily fights *were* the fun).
- **Streaks:** Duolingo's streak system (milestones at 7/30/100/365 days, streak freezes as buffer) roughly doubles daily retention; loss-aversion on cumulative rewards works but needs catch-up mechanics to avoid burnout-quit.
- **Seasons/battle passes:** dominant engagement layer; seasonal resets refresh ladders and content cadence.
- **Guild obligations:** social commitment (Torn factions, AFK Journey guilds) is the strongest D30+ driver — people return for *people*.

## 5. Ethical monetisation benchmarks

**Battle pass norms:** ~$10 per season is the anchor (Halo Infinite: 1,000 credits = $10 for a 100-tier pass with free + premium tracks; 2,800 credits with +25 tiers). Two player-loved deviations:
- **Retroactive/permanent passes:** Halo Infinite made *all* passes permanently purchasable and grindable ("available indefinitely") — widely praised, with the caveat that only premium owners can progress an old pass after its Operation ends.
- **Deep Rock Galactic's free Performance Pass:** 100% free, cosmetic, ~100 hours per pass, and past seasons can be toggled on/off at will — the community explicitly credits it as the anti-FOMO gold standard ("they aren't counting on FOMO to keep players playing").

**Cosmetic-only economies that worked:** Path of Exile funds a AAA-scale live game on cosmetics + **supporter packs** (bundles from ~$30 up to $12,000+, framed as "keep us making more of the same"); trust is the asset — players don't feel compelled to spend to compete. Super Auto Pets is the small-scale equivalent (packs + earnable cosmetics + free weekly rotation). Deep Rock sells cosmetic DLC alongside the free pass.

**Supporter subscriptions:** Torn's $4.85/mo donator subscription (status + monthly items + points, *tradeable* packs) and Melvor's buy-once + expansions show two clean models for small teams. Both monetise *appreciation and convenience*, not power.

**P2W vs acceptable convenience — the community line, drawn from the cases above:**
- Acceptable: cosmetics; account-wide QoL (extra character slots, loadouts, offline-progress length); content expansions *if* baseline competition is unaffected (Super Auto Pets gets grumbles even here); time-savers with hard caps; supporter status badges.
- Radioactive: paying for ranked entry (The Bazaar's $1/run — instant revolt), power/content locked in a paid monthly pass (Bazaar Prize Pass), energy systems that split F2P/payer progression 1:3 (Shakes & Fidget's 100 vs 300 energy), premium-currency-priced core items.

**Regulatory tailwind against loot boxes:** Belgium bans paid loot boxes outright (2018 Gaming Commission ruling; 2025 Antwerp court confirmed); the Netherlands treats paid loot boxes granting advancement as unlawful and in Dec 2024 its Economic Affairs Minister formally urged an **EU-wide ban**; the EU Digital Fairness Act (expected from late 2025) is likely to add loot-box consumer-protection rules; a 2024 UNICEF-cited UK figure of 92,000 children regretting purchases fuels sentiment. Design implication: *no paid randomness at all* — it's both ethically and legally the safe corridor for an EU-facing browser game.

## 6. The opportunity gap

Mapping the field exposes a hole no one currently occupies:

1. **2-minute sessions + real build identity.** Super Auto Pets/Backpack Battles/The Bazaar added drafting agency but demand 10–40 minute runs and reset your build every run. MyBrute had a *persistent* character but zero agency. **Nobody offers a persistent, named, evolving fighter you shape through meaningful choices and check on in two minutes.** That combination — roguelike build-craft applied to a *permanent* character on a ladder — is unclaimed.
2. **Social lineage as viral engine.** MyBrute's pupil/master referral tree is still the best organic-growth mechanic the genre ever produced, and none of the modern auto-battlers use it. Discord Activities + Telegram Mini Apps are tailor-made distribution for lineage links (a brute link *is* a share object).
3. **Spectatable async fights.** Modern ghost battles are private. MyBrute fights were URLs anyone could watch. Replay links + Discord embeds = free content marketing every fight.
4. **Trust-first monetisation as positioning.** The Bazaar burned the genre's goodwill in public; Super Auto Pets, Melvor, Torn, DRG and PoE prove the alternative prints loyalty. A stated "no paid power, no paid randomness, retroactive passes, tradeable supporter perks" charter is now a *marketable feature*, not just ethics.
5. **The web itself.** Instant load in a portal iframe, a Discord call, a Telegram chat and a PWA — a lightweight 2D auto-battler is the ideal shape for the 95M+ MAU portal audience plus Discord's 200M, where mid-core competitors (AFK Journey et al.) cannot follow without rebuilding.

**Recommended benchmark targets:** D1 ≥ 35%, D7 ≥ 15%, D30 ≥ 8%; monetisation via ~$5/mo supporter sub (Torn-style, ideally gift/tradeable) + ~$10 seasonal *retroactive* cosmetic pass (DRG/Halo hybrid) + cosmetic packs; zero loot boxes; free ranked play forever.
